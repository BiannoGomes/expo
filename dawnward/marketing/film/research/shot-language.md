# Dawnward v4: Shot Language

A distilled grammar of shots and transitions for the launch film, derived from the reference library (see reference-library.md for sources and verification status). Every pattern names the reference it came from. These are principles for original execution, not shot lists to copy.

House constraints that govern every pattern below: near-black #0B0E12 canvas, ivory #ECE9E2 type in Cormorant Garamond, gold #C9A96A used scarcely (one accent per frame at most), no gamified motion, nothing loud.

---

## 1. Opening patterns

### 1.1 The held black
- Source: Calm's silent election-night spot (restraint as positioning).
- Pattern: open on true near-black (#0B0E12) with near-silence for one to two seconds before any image or word. The film begins by withholding. In a feed, stillness against autoplay noise is the pattern interrupt.
- Note: time the first appearing element precisely; the held black only works if what breaks it is worth the wait.

### 1.2 One ordinary human moment, taken seriously
- Source: Apple "Little Garlic" (centring a recognisable insecurity).
- Pattern: the first photographed image is a small, specific, true moment of the gap between intention and behaviour: an unopened journal, a thumb hovering, an alarm going quiet. Shot close, warm, unhurried. No logo, no UI, no voice yet.
- Note: specificity is the craft. "A person on a sofa" is generic; "a half-typed sentence deleted at 11:04pm" is recognised.

### 1.3 The repeated gesture
- Source: Apple "Get Ready" (one gesture repeated until the product completes it).
- Pattern: if v4 uses montage at all, it is three to five original shots of the same micro-gesture (a pause, a breath, a hand settling) across different people or times of day, with the final instance belonging to the product moment. Never borrowed footage, never more than five beats, never fast.

## 2. Reveal mechanics

### 2.1 Ordinary space transforms, anchor stays
- Source: Calm "Sleep Stories: The Orient Express" (bedroom becomes train, bedroom remains bedroom); Apple "Introducing Apple Vision Pro" (real room persists through imagined expansion).
- Pattern: keep one real anchor constant (window light, a hand, a cup) while the surrounding frame gradually takes on the Dawnward world: shadows deepen towards #0B0E12, one gold glint appears where dawn light would strike. The room never stops being the room. The product's effect is shown as a change of atmosphere, not a change of location.

### 2.2 The match-cut reveal
- Source: Nike "You Can't Stop Us" (movement continuity across the cut, eye-matched and planned in-camera).
- Pattern: a human micro-movement completes as an interface movement. The pen stroke's direction, speed and screen position continue as a gold line drawing itself in the UI; an exhale's fall continues as a card settling. Plan at storyboard stage: matching is a shooting decision, not a post fix.
- Budget: one, at most two, match cuts in the film. Scarcity keeps them felt rather than noticed.

### 2.3 The product as the quietest thing in the film
- Source: Apple "Get Ready" (product appears once, last, as the only new image); Calm election spot (the brand administers its promise).
- Pattern: when Dawnward's UI first appears, drop the sound floor and slow the motion rather than raising them. The reveal is a settling, not an arrival. Nothing in the film after the reveal moves faster than before it.

## 3. UI close-up patterns

### 3.1 Floating surfaces on the void
- Source: Notion "Introducing the new Notion AI" (real recordings composited into floating UI cards; unreviewed at frame level, per listing description).
- Pattern: real Dawnward screens presented as composed surfaces floating on the near-black field, slightly oversized, cropped with intent. Never a full phone frame filling the screen; never fake content. One card per shot where possible.

### 3.2 Typography as protagonist
- Source: Notion launch film genre (typography-led motion); BUCK's Notion systems (motion personality for a software feature).
- Pattern: a word set in Cormorant Garamond is the subject of the shot. It is typed, breathed onto the canvas, or resolves from a human context into UI. Letterforms move like set type, not like motion-graphics toys: opacity and position eases, no bounces, no per-letter gymnastics.

### 3.3 UI that behaves like a presence
- Source: BUCK "Notion AI Assistant" (state-machine character with blended emotional states).
- Pattern: give Dawnward's interface one consistent motion temperament across every close-up: slow ease-ins, long settles, a sense that the UI waits for the person rather than prompting them. Define the easing curves once and reuse them in every shot, so the product has a recognisable way of moving.

### 3.4 Material truth on the dark canvas
- Source: ManvsMachine Nike work (3D scanning for product fidelity; abstract CG with physically plausible light).
- Pattern: any abstract element (particles, constellation lines, gold threads) must obey plausible light: it should pick up the scene's key, cast faint glow onto nearby surfaces, and have material character (ink, dust, metal leaf) rather than reading as a screen overlay.

## 4. Depth and parallax patterns

### 4.1 The slow push through layers
- Source: Ordinary Folk (slow camera pushes through layered 2.5D scenes with gradient-rich light).
- Pattern: camera moves are slow dolly pushes or drifts, never whips or orbit flourishes. Build frames in three to four depth layers (foreground shadow, subject, mid atmosphere, far glow) so a small push creates parallax and the dark frame feels dimensional rather than flat.

### 4.2 Real-to-imagined depth hand-off
- Source: Apple "Introducing Apple Vision Pro" (real space extends into imagined space without a cut).
- Pattern: let photographed depth hand over to composed depth inside one move: the push past a real object (door frame, curtain, shoulder) wipes naturally into the near-black canvas where the UI lives, and back out again at the end.

### 4.3 Interior landscape drift
- Source: Antibody title sequences (double-exposure compositing, slow inevitable drift, dark palette with one luminous accent).
- Pattern: for the film's most emblematic image, composite a human silhouette or profile with a landscape of the person's own progress (faint gold constellation of past reflections) occupying the same frame, and drift through it slowly. One luminous accent only; the rest stays within two stops of the canvas black.

## 5. Ending patterns

### 5.1 Resolve to stillness
- Source: Calm election spot (silence as the brand's proof); Apple Vision Pro films (calm confidence rather than crescendo).
- Pattern: the final ten percent of the film decelerates. End on a held, almost-still frame: the wordmark or a single line of Cormorant on near-black, with one gold detail. No end-card stack, no feature list, no urgency device.

### 5.2 The completed gesture
- Source: Apple "Get Ready" (the ad's repeated gesture finds its final instance); Nike "You Can't Stop Us" (payoff earned by every previous beat restating one idea).
- Pattern: the closing image answers the opening image. If the film opens on the unopened journal at night, it closes on the same frame at dawn with one line written. Symmetry does the persuading; the voice says at most one quiet sentence.

### 5.3 Repeatable sign-off motif
- Source: Google "Best Phones Forever" (fixed campaign grammar so every variant deposits equity in the same place).
- Pattern: design the last three seconds (the settle of the wordmark, its timing, its sound) as a fixed, reusable motif identical across the hero film, cutdowns and future campaigns. This is the unit of recognition for everything Dawnward ships afterwards.

---

## Usage rules

- One idea per shot. If a frame needs two reads, split it.
- Gold appears in a frame only when it means something (first light, a completed line, the sign-off).
- No shot imitates a referenced film's recognisable device (Nike's sustained split screen, Calm's silent-buy stunt, Notion's illustration language). Patterns above are principles; executions must be original.
- Any pattern derived from an item marked "unreviewed at frame level" in reference-library.md inherits that caveat: verify against the actual film before citing it in client-facing materials.
