# Dawnward Launch Film v4: Reference Library

Phase 1 research notes. Internal production use only. Techniques are extracted, never assets. All references were researched through written teardowns, case studies, press analyses and official documentation; no video was watched directly. Where a frame-level claim could not be verified from written sources, the item is marked "unreviewed at frame level" and the supporting secondary sources are named. No copyrighted copy lines are reproduced here. Campaign metrics quoted from award entries are self-reported by the brands and are not independently verified.

Dawnward identity, for reading these notes: near-black #0B0E12, ivory #ECE9E2, scarce gold #C9A96A, Cormorant Garamond serif, the voice of a caring person, never gamified, never loud.

## 1. Apple Vision Pro suite

### 1a. Apple "Get Ready" (January 2024)

- URL: https://uploadvr.com/apple-vision-pro-get-ready-ad (coverage); https://9to5mac.com/2024/01/08/vision-pro-ad-movie-clips/ (clip inventory); https://petapixel.com/2024/01/09/apples-vision-pro-ad-wants-to-make-it-cool-to-wear-a-headset (analysis)
- Status: unreviewed at frame level. Supported by UploadVR, 9to5Mac, PetaPixel, AppleInsider and iMore coverage, which agree on structure and content.
- What happens (as verifiable): a roughly 30 to 39 second montage (sources disagree on length) of licensed film and TV clips in which beloved characters put something on their head or face: goggles, helmets, masks, visors. Sources name Young Frankenstein, Up, Star Wars, Star Trek, Iron Man, Back to the Future and others. The final shot is a real person putting on Vision Pro, revealing the visionOS home screen in passthrough. Widely read as a deliberate callback to the 2007 iPhone "Hello" teaser.
- What the sound contributes: the spot is cut to an energetic licensed track (Devo, "Uncontrollable Urge" per coverage). The music carries continuity across wildly different source footage; the repeated physical gesture carries the idea.
- Why the technique works: one repeated gesture, shown many times with escalating familiarity, primes a single action (putting the product on) before the product appears. The product reveal is the only "new" image in the film, so it lands with full weight.
- Suits Dawnward? Partially. The structural idea (one repeated human gesture, product as the final instance of it) suits Dawnward well. The borrowed-clips montage and the loud energy do not.
- Original translation: build a quiet montage of one original, filmable gesture (a person pausing: hand on a journal, thumb hovering over a phone, a breath before sleep) and let the Dawnward UI be the final, calmest instance of that pause.
- Do not copy: licensed film clips, the fast comedic rhythm, any nostalgia-collage structure, the song.

### 1b. "Introducing Apple Vision Pro" (June 2023)

- URL: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2023/06/introducing-apple-vision-pro/ (official announcement); https://www.uploadvr.com/vision-pro-apple-most-watched-youtube-video/ (reach)
- Status: unreviewed at frame level. Supported by Apple's newsroom description and third-party commentary; UploadVR reported it became the most-watched public video on Apple's YouTube channel (just under 51 million views at time of its writing).
- What happens (as verifiable): the launch film presents "spatial computing": the wearer sees the real room with apps floating in it, then scenes move between real domestic space and expanded or imagined space (large virtual cinema screens, immersive environments, FaceTime Personas). Commentary notes the film does the persuading for a very expensive product, and that the pacing is deliberate and calm.
- What the sound contributes: cannot be verified from written sources at frame level; commentary describes the overall register as calm and confident rather than hyped.
- Why the technique works: real-to-imagined-space transitions keep one anchor constant (the room, the person, the light) while everything around it changes, so the magic reads as credible rather than fantastical. Generous negative space around UI keeps a dense product legible and premium.
- Suits Dawnward? Strongly. Dawnward's near-black canvas is itself negative space. A real bedroom or desk at dawn, with the app's ivory type arriving into that darkness, is the same grammar at a smaller scale.
- Original translation: hold one real anchor (a window at first light, a cup, a hand) while the space around it quietly becomes the Dawnward canvas; let UI elements occupy a small fraction of a mostly empty frame.
- Do not copy: Apple's product-rotation hero shots, the white-studio look, the "era of" announcement framing, visionOS interface imagery.

### 1c. Apple Vision Pro "Guided Tour" (January 2024)

- URL: https://briansolis.com/2024/01/a-guided-tour-of-apples-vision-pro-and-a-glimpse-of-the-future-of-spatial-computing/ (analysis)
- Status: unreviewed at frame level. Supported by Brian Solis's written walkthrough analysis.
- What happens (as verifiable): a long-form, host-led walkthrough that teaches the product's interactions step by step. Solis notes the calm, simple instructions from the host were deliberate: the film prepares viewers for a new kind of experience and manages expectation rather than selling features.
- What the sound contributes: a single warm human voice carries nearly the whole film; this is the core technique, not an accompaniment.
- Why the technique works: a patient, unhurried teacher voice converts anxiety about newness into anticipated competence. It demonstrates desire through understanding, not spectacle.
- Suits Dawnward? Yes, for the companion or long-cut asset more than the hero film. Dawnward's "voice of a caring person" is exactly this register.
- Original translation: a 60 to 90 second quiet guided moment (one evening reflection, start to finish, in real time) voiced like someone who knows you, not a presenter.
- Do not copy: the host-on-couch staging, Apple's instructional phrasing, the product-demo structure as a whole for the hero film (too long, too literal).

## 2. Nike "You Can't Stop Us" (2020, Wieden+Kennedy Portland)

- URL: https://magazine.workingnotworking.com/creative-work-blog/you-cant-stop-us-nike-ad (maker interview; site was unreachable at research time, cited from search summary); https://www.creativereview.co.uk/nike-you-cant-stop-us/ (analysis); https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/oscar-hudson-wieden-kennedy-nike-you-cant-stop-us-advertising-060820 (process)
- Status: unreviewed at frame level for individual cuts. Process facts are well supported by multiple independent trade write-ups.
- What happens (as verifiable): a split-screen film directed by Oscar Hudson (Pulse Films) with VFX by A52 and editorial by JOINT. The team reviewed roughly 4,000 sports action sequences and kept 72, combined into 36 split-screen moments (counts vary slightly by outlet: one cites 78 clips, another 24 sports and 53 athletes). Left and right halves show different athletes whose movement matches across the centre seam; action flows continuously across pairs while a voiceover builds from individual struggle to collective resolve.
- What the sound contributes: per coverage, a steadily building voiceover provides the escalation spine; the edit rhythm is locked to the speech cadence. The craft story emphasises picture matching, with sound as the emotional throughline.
- Why the technique works: the match cut across the seam makes "difference" and "sameness" one image. Movement continuity across cuts creates momentum without speed, and the escalation earns its payoff because every pair restates one idea.
- How it was made (relevant craft): editors eye-matched shots rather than using automated tools; new footage was shot specifically to match archive framing so the effect was largely achieved in-camera. The lesson: match cuts are a casting and planning problem, not a post trick.
- Suits Dawnward? The match-cut grammar yes, the anthemic scale no. Dawnward can match movement across a seam quietly: a hand writing and a line drawing itself, a breath and a UI element settling.
- Original translation: one or two precise match cuts where a human micro-movement (exhale, pen stroke, eyes closing) continues as a motion inside the Dawnward interface. Plan them at the storyboard stage so motion direction, speed and screen position genuinely match.
- Do not copy: the split-screen seam as a sustained device (it is now strongly associated with this film), sports footage, montage escalation to a roaring finale, any voiceover echoing its rhetoric.

## 3. Calm app advertising

### 3a. Calm's silent election-night spot (November 2024)

- URL: https://www.adweek.com/creativity/five-takeaways-from-calms-election-night-ad/ ; https://www.fastcompany.com/91222291/calm-app-election-night-ad-silence
- Status: unreviewed at frame level. Supported by Adweek, Fast Company, Marketing Brew and The Desk coverage.
- Naming note: the brief lists this as "Do Nothing for 30 Seconds". Verified coverage describes Calm's 2024 spot as 30 seconds of silence bought during election-night coverage on CNN and ABC (some outlets add Comedy Central), with on-screen text acknowledging the silence and closing on the Calm logo. "Do nothing" phrasing appears in related coverage of other brands' low-stimulation ads (Marketing Brew groups Calm with Whisker and Angel Soft). Treated here as the same strategic family; the exact "Do Nothing for 30 Seconds" title is unreviewed.
- What happens (as verifiable): near-total silence and stillness in the most anxious, loudest media slot of the year, closing with a light acknowledgement and the logo. Coverage reports a large social-mention spike (one analysis cites a 248 percent rise in Twitter mentions; treat as third-party and directional).
- What the sound contributes: the absence of sound is the entire ad. Context does the work: silence reads as relief only because everything around it is noise.
- Why the technique works: strategic restraint is positioning made physical. The brand does not describe calm, it administers it. Low density also survives sound-off feeds, where most ads die.
- Suits Dawnward? Strongly, as a principle. Dawnward should be the quietest thing in any feed it appears in.
- Original translation: open the film with one to two seconds of true black and near-silence before anything happens; build a cutdown whose first three seconds contain a single ivory word on near-black and no motion, designed to stop a scroll by contrast.
- Do not copy: the stunt itself (a silent media buy is Calm's owned move now), the self-aware caption device, any "you're welcome" tonality.

### 3b. Calm "Sleep Stories: The Orient Express" (2018 TV spot)

- URL: https://www.ispot.tv/ad/dMnJ/calm-sleep-stories-the-orient-express
- Status: unreviewed at frame level. Supported by the iSpot.tv listing description and National Geographic's piece on Calm's travel-themed sleep content (https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/why-do-travel-tales-put-people-to-sleep).
- What happens (as verifiable): a woman opens the app and selects the Orient Express sleep story; as she drifts off, her ordinary bedroom is gradually transformed into the scene of a train crossing Italy. Published September 2018 per the listing.
- What the sound contributes: cannot be verified at frame level; the format implies a narrated story and ambient train textures replacing room tone.
- Why the technique works: ordinary-space transformation shows the product's effect, not its screens. The bedroom stays recognisable while the imagined world grows around it, which keeps the promise intimate and believable.
- Suits Dawnward? Strongly. Dawnward's product effect (a day slowly reorienting around intention) is invisible; transforming an ordinary space is the honest way to film an interior change.
- Original translation: a dim room at 5:50am where, as a reflection is typed, elements of the room pick up faint gold accents of first light; the room never stops being the room.
- Do not copy: the train imagery, the literal story-world set dressing, any sleep-specific framing (Dawnward is about becoming, not sleeping).

## 4. Google Pixel "Best Phones Forever" (2023 onward, Google Creative Lab with LAD)

- URL: https://lad.studio/bpf (case study, verified live at research time); https://shortyawards.com/16th/bestphonesforever (award entry); https://www.droid-life.com/2023/06/21/google-pixel-bestphonesforever-iphone-commercials/ (launch coverage)
- Status: unreviewed at frame level for individual spots. Format and process well supported by the LAD case study, Shorty and Cannes entries, and trade coverage.
- What happens (as verifiable): a long-running series of short films in which a Pixel and an iPhone, as two phone characters, chat as best friends; the humour rests on three rules per Google's Cannes entry: place iPhone beside Pixel as a viable alternative, respect iPhone as the category definer, celebrate Pixel strengths that cannot be refuted. LAD (Brooklyn) supported Google Creative Lab with art direction, design and animation; The Mill handled 3D, VFX and colour; Heard did sound. The series launched June 2023 with five films and kept generating variants for years. Google's self-reported Shorty figures: a 191 percent rise in search activity, 8x owned-social follower growth, a 2.12-point consideration lift (unverified, self-reported).
- What the sound contributes: two consistent character voices are the campaign's recognisable sonic motif; the same voices across every variant make each new spot instantly identifiable before any logo appears.
- Why the technique works: a fixed, cheap-to-repeat format (two characters, one location grammar, one tonal register) turns a campaign into a system. Every variant deposits equity in the same place. Recognition arrives in the first second.
- Suits Dawnward? The system thinking yes, the comedy and competitor-baiting no. Dawnward v4 should be designed as the first instance of a repeatable visual grammar, not a one-off.
- Original translation: define three fixed Dawnward motifs (the near-black canvas with one gold accent, the Cormorant word that resolves into UI, a recurring sonic signature) so every future cutdown and variant is recognisably the same world within one second.
- Do not copy: anthropomorphised products, competitor references, comedic dialogue, the sitcom cadence.

## 5. Notion "Introducing the new Notion AI" launch film

- URL: https://echai.ventures/videos/notion-introducing-the-new-notion-ai (listing); related Notion motion craft: https://buck.co/work/notion-ai and https://buck.co/work/notion-think-it-make-it
- Status: unreviewed at frame level. Written analysis of this specific film is thin; the eChai listing describes it, and BUCK's Notion case pages document the adjacent craft system. Claims kept minimal accordingly.
- What happens (as verifiable): per the listing, a 2024 launch film built as one continuous screen-led demonstration: real product recordings composited into floating UI cards, showing the assistant pulling answers from Notion, Slack and Drive. Adjacent verified craft: BUCK evolved Notion AI's face from a static drawing into a fluid cel-animated character using Rive state machines (buck.co/work/notion-ai), and built the "Think it. Make it." animated anthem system (buck.co/work/notion-think-it-make-it).
- What the sound contributes: cannot be verified at frame level. The genre convention this film sits in (music-led SaaS launch films) paces cuts to a track rather than to voiceover; treat that as a genre observation, not a verified fact about this film.
- Why the technique works: floating UI cards on a clean field make software readable at feed sizes; typography-led motion lets the written word be the protagonist, which suits products whose substance is text; one continuous demonstration builds more trust than stitched fragments.
- Suits Dawnward? Strongly, for the UI passages. Dawnward is also a text-first product, and Cormorant Garamond deserves to be the lead actor.
- Original translation: show real Dawnward UI as composed, floating surfaces on the near-black field with real (non-fabricated) content; let a typed word animate into an interface element in one unbroken move.
- Do not copy: Notion's monochrome illustration language, its UI chrome, the demo-tour structure at full length, any cursor-choreography clichés.

## 6. Apple "Little Garlic" (Chinese New Year 2024, TBWA\Media Arts Lab Shanghai)

- URL: https://campaignbrief.com/apple-releases-highly-anticipated-chinese-new-year-film-via-tbwamedia-arts-lab-shanghai/ ; https://www.storyboard18.com/quantum-brief/apples-chinese-new-year-film-explores-identity-and-social-media-21351.htm ; https://shots.net/news/view/apples-shapeshifting-celebration-of-chinese-new-year
- Status: unreviewed at frame level. Story, credits and production facts consistent across Campaign Brief, Storyboard18, shots and afaqs.
- What happens (as verifiable): a 15-minute Shot on iPhone film directed by Marc Webb on iPhone 15 Pro Max (DP Cecile Zhang; production Radical US and M Production Shanghai; seventh annual CNY film). A girl is self-conscious about the broad nose her grandfather affectionately nicknames; she gains the ability to shape-shift into more attractive people, and the gift becomes a burden as social-media pressure grows. The resolution is self-acceptance. Apple's marketing lead praised how the technology fades so the story's humanity stands out. The "shot entirely on iPhone" claim is Apple's own framing.
- What the sound contributes: cannot be verified at frame level from written sources.
- Why the technique works: the film centres one small, recognisable insecurity and takes it completely seriously. The product is never the subject; it is the invisible witness. Human truth creates desire by association, not demonstration.
- Suits Dawnward? Strongly, as the emotional model. Dawnward's audience carries a quiet insecurity: the gap between who they are at 11pm and who they meant to be at 7am. Naming that gap gently, without shame, is the Little Garlic move.
- Original translation: open v4 on one specific, filmable instance of that gap (an unopened journal, a snoozed alarm, a reflection half-typed and deleted) and treat it with warmth, never judgement; the app arrives as the caring witness.
- Do not copy: the fantasy shape-shifting conceit, the family-holiday setting, the 15-minute short-film ambition, any "shot on" framing.

## 7. Studios worth studying

### 7a. BUCK, https://buck.co (verified live at research time)

- Pieces noted: "Notion AI Assistant" (https://buck.co/work/notion-ai): evolving a static drawing into a fluid cel-animated character, with Rive state machines blending expressions between states. "Notion: Think it. Make it." (https://buck.co/work/notion-think-it-make-it): an animated anthem system for AI feature headlines. Current homepage work includes J.P. Morgan Payments "Creative Expression" and Kia "AI Assistant" identity.
- Technique worth studying: giving a software feature a consistent, living motion personality; cel-smooth transitions between emotional states; building motion systems (not one-off films) that a brand team can reuse. Directly relevant to how Dawnward's UI elements should "behave" like a calm presence rather than animate like widgets.

### 7b. Ordinary Folk, https://ordinaryfolk.co (verified live at research time)

- Pieces noted: "Distance" (original short), "Google Montage" (client: Google), "CryptoCubes", plus originals "Equilibrium" and "Rio 40°". Vancouver motion design and animation studio.
- Technique worth studying: lush, gradient-rich lighting inside flat design; slow camera pushes through layered 2.5D scenes; letting a single colour temperature carry mood across a whole film. Relevant to making Dawnward's near-black and gold feel lit, not just coloured.

### 7c. ManvsMachine, https://mvsm.com (verified live at research time; portfolio detail from secondary sources)

- Pieces noted (via https://www.stashmedia.tv/manvsmachine-nike-air-max-2017/ , https://maxon.net/en/article/man-vs-machine-fusing-creativity-with-technology-with-the-help-of-cinema-4d , https://www.creativereview.co.uk/nike-air-max-kids-brand-identity/): Nike Air Max 2017 (an abstract CG film the studio described as a metaphorical exploration of air and negative space), Air Max Day 2016 kinetic-machine campaign, Nike Air Max Kids inflatable 3D brand system (Cannes shortlisted).
- Technique worth studying: material truth in CG (they 3D-scan real products so renders match reality), abstract metaphor standing in for product benefit, choreographed camera moves through sparse space. Pipeline per Maxon interview: Cinema 4D with Arnold and V-Ray, Houdini for complex work, After Effects and Nuke compositing. Relevant to any abstract "constellation" or particle sequences in Dawnward v4: materials and lighting must feel physically plausible on the dark canvas.

### 7d. Antibody, https://antibody.tv (verified live at research time)

- Pieces noted: main titles for True Detective (HBO), Westworld Season 4 (HBO), 3 Body Problem (Netflix), Silo (Apple TV+), The Crown (Netflix); originals including "The Phisherman's Daughter". Directors Patrick Clair and Raoul Marks; multiple Emmy nominations and wins noted on site.
- Technique worth studying: double-exposure compositing, where landscapes and figures occupy each other; slow, inevitable camera drift; symbolic imagery that rewards rewatching; dark palettes with one luminous accent. This is the closest existing grammar to "a person's interior landscape rendered in near-black and gold" and the most directly transferable aesthetic reference for Dawnward.

## 8. Sound tools and licensing

### 8a. ElevenLabs "Introducing Instant Soundtracks"

- URL: https://elevenlabs.io/blog/introducing-instant-soundtracks
- What it is: a feature (in ElevenCreative, ElevenMusic and the API) that generates original music for an uploaded video, then scans the footage and places sound effects on the timeline at matching timestamps (their example: a whoosh timed to an end logo). Effects sit on separate layers in the editor, so music and SFX are independently adjustable. It can use the video's existing audio as a reference for the new score and handles scene-to-scene mood changes and short-form pacing. Limits noted in localised versions of the post: up to 10 videos per request, 200 MB total, 10 minutes of video.
- Relevance: a fast way to prototype v4's sonic identity against picture before commissioning or finalising; the separate-layers output suits Dawnward's need for near-silence with isolated accents.

### 8b. Eleven Music documentation

- URL: https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/music (see also https://elevenlabs.io/blog/eleven-music-is-here)
- Capabilities: tracks from 3 seconds to 5 minutes; vocal or instrumental; natural-language or music-terminology prompts; section-level editing and inpainting (regenerate one section without touching the rest); Music v2 adds long-form section-by-section composition and mid-track genre transitions; audio-reference guidance from an uploaded clip of about 30 seconds (paid plans); custom finetunes on non-copyrighted material; MP3 and WAV output; API for paid subscribers.
- Commercial terms (general): ElevenLabs states the model was built through rights-holder partnerships (coverage names Merlin Network and Kobalt) and is cleared for nearly all commercial uses including advertising; scope varies by plan, and some uses (film and TV per the API post) require Enterprise. Before using any generated track in a paid Dawnward ad, verify the current terms at elevenlabs.io/music-terms for the plan in use. Do not treat this file as a licence summary.

### 8c. ElevenLabs sound effects documentation

- URL: https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/sound-effects
- Capabilities: text-to-SFX up to 30 seconds per generation (settable 0.1 to 30 seconds); prompt-influence control from literal to creative; seamless looping for atmospheres and ambient beds; sequenced prompts for multi-part effects; can generate musical components (drum loops, bass lines); MP3, plus 48 kHz WAV for non-looping effects. The docs page states no licensing terms; commercial scope must be confirmed against the account's plan terms before paid-media use.

### 8d. Artlist and Epidemic Sound (licensed libraries) for paid ads

- URLs: https://wyzowl.com/artlist-vs-epidemic-sound/ ; https://www.epidemicsound.com/how-it-works/how-we-compare/ (vendor marketing)
- General picture from third-party comparisons (figures vary between sources and date quickly; verify on each vendor's licence page before purchase): Artlist sells commercial access as yearly plans, with its higher tier described as covering client work, broadcast and commercial video. Epidemic Sound's commercial tier is monthly and described as covering digital ads, with broadcast and TV clearance in an enterprise tier; account and seat caps apply.
- What paid Dawnward ads would require: a licence tier that explicitly covers paid digital advertising (not just organic social or YouTube monetisation), coverage for the entities running the ads (brand account, any agency seats), and broadcast or CTV clearance if the media plan extends there. Sources conflict on whether Artlist licensing is per-project or unlimited across media, which is exactly the kind of clause to confirm in the current licence text. Keep licence PDFs with the project archive for every track used.
