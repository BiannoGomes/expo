# Dawnward launch film: reference board

Research for a 60-second vertical (9:16) product film for Dawnward. Compiled 30 October 2026.

## How to read this

- **I could not watch any video.** Every observation comes from the studios' own case-study text, credits, the image alt text on those pages, press coverage, or still frames published on the studio pages. Where a still is cited, I downloaded and looked at that exact frame.
- **Confidence levels.** *High* means a source states it, or I saw it in a published still. *Medium* means it is consistent across several descriptions or stills but nobody states it outright. *Low* means it is my inference.
- **Software.** No software is named unless a source names it. None of the sources below name animation software. BUCK's page says the iMac Pro film was made on iMac Pros, which is hardware.
- **Sources I could not read.** The Tavily extract quota was exhausted. LBB Online and Creative Review returned 403. Those articles are not cited.
- **Studio rule.** Every adaptation borrows a principle only. No shot sequence, logo, footage, character, music or signature visual gag is to be copied.

## 1. *Distance*, Ordinary Folk

- **Client:** none. This is a studio passion project. The script comes from Nate DiMeo's *The Memory Palace* (2012), and DiMeo is executive producer.
- **Year:** 2026. Runtime 3:26.
- **Opened:** https://ordinaryfolk.co/project/distance (case-study page with alt text, timeline and credits) and https://ordinaryfolk.co/work (index).
- **Stills viewed:** "two hands reaching across the dark", "an envelope in a beam of light", "a window of light in black blocks", "a line of Morse code".
- **Supporting reference:** https://www.ordinaryfolk.co/project/gemini (Google *Gemini*, 2024, 1:14). The studio says it animated text "in a meaningful way" while showing the product, in a "bold, graphic" style. This page has little detail, so it is only a supporting note.

### Techniques

**1. Light is the subject; black is the ground.**
- Observation: Light carries the story. An envelope sits where a pale beam bends across a black field. A white square of "window" is surrounded by black and grey ink blocks. A hand-made light table and copy stand photographed every frame.
- Source: stills 01239 and 03663 (seen); alt text "an envelope in a beam of light" and "city windows at night".
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: In a dark field, the eye goes straight to the brightest area (an orienting response). Light becomes both where you look and what the film means: hope, contact, a message arriving.

**2. The gap is the composition.**
- Observation: Two cut-paper-like hands reach toward each other from the frame edges and do not touch. The empty black between them takes up the centre of the frame. In another frame, a single short line of Morse code sits small and centred on a large off-white field.
- Source: stills 00401 and 04214 (seen); the page describes the film as being about "illusions of space and distance".
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: An unresolved gap creates anticipation, and the viewer mentally closes it (Gestalt closure). Large negative space slows reading and makes a small element feel important.

**3. A visible human hand, monochrome by choice.**
- Observation: More than 900 frames were painted by hand, then photographed, so brush drag, dry-brush streaks, paper grain and ragged edges show. The style explorations include "a painted eye in colour" next to "a painted eye in greys". The frames I saw are all ink greys.
- Source: page text and process photos (alt text); stills seen.
- Confidence: high for the hand-painting. That colour was explored is also high (alt text). That colour was deliberately dropped is medium: it is inferred from the final frames.
- Psychological function: Visible effort reads as care (the effort heuristic). Imperfection signals that a person made it, which builds trust in a way synthetic smoothness does not.

### For Dawnward

- **Fits:** Near-black ground with light as the protagonist matches Dawnward's system exactly. The film's theme is two people communicating across a distance, which parallels "it understands you". The restraint (one tone, lots of space) suits "calm, premium".
- **Does not fit:** The full 3:26 painterly-art-film runtime. Hand-painted frames are not product UI, and Dawnward must show its real screens. There is no warm gold in it; it is all cool greys.
- **Original adaptation, "the lit gap":** Put the person's own debrief sentence on the left third and Dawnward's insight on the right third, with clean dark space between them. While the "Why I think this" sheet opens (real UI, real copy), a single thin warm line of light crosses the gap, from the words to the reason. This borrows the principle that distance is the subject and light is the bridge. It copies no hands, envelope or Morse code.

## 2. Calm Sleep Visual System, BUCK

- **Client:** Calm (Calm Sleep app).
- **Year:** not stated on the page (the © 2026 is the site footer).
- **Opened:** https://buck.co/work/calm-sleep-visual-system (page text and credits). I also downloaded the page HTML to find its own stills.
- **Stills viewed:** six. They include a figure seen from behind, a face with eyes closed among glowing moon-phase discs, a mirrored gradient horizon, a hand holding a glass of water, and concentric gradient rings.
- **Note:** This is a visual system with a sizzle reel, not one ad film.

### Techniques

**1. Light behaviour carries the system, not decoration.**
- Observation: In the stills, soft glows, halos and luminous discs do the storytelling. A ring of glowing moon phases circles a sleeping face, and a light source blooms behind a figure.
- Source: page text: "ethereal lighting behaviors" and "gradients that frame and tell stories". Stills seen.
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: Soft, diffuse light with low local contrast lowers arousal. Light that changes over time reads as a state (falling asleep, settling) rather than an object.

**2. It moves between metaphor and literal on purpose.**
- Observation: The page says the system had to move "fluidly from metaphorical to literal use cases". The stills show both: a literal hand holding a glass of water, and abstract mirrored horizons and concentric rings that read as breath or sleep.
- Source: page text; stills seen.
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: Literal frames anchor comprehension ("this is about drinking water before bed"). Metaphoric frames carry feeling. Alternating the two keeps poetry from tipping into vagueness.

**3. Calm, faceless figures in centred, symmetrical frames.**
- Observation: Figures are seen from behind or with eyes closed, and features are minimal. The gradient compositions are centred and often mirrored top to bottom. A fine grain overlays the smooth gradients.
- Source: stills seen.
- Confidence: high for these frames. That this holds across the whole system is medium: I saw six of about 28 images.
- Psychological function: A closed-eyed or turned-away figure models the target state, so the viewer imitates it rather than watching a performance. Viewers project themselves onto faceless figures. Symmetry reads as stability.

### For Dawnward

- **Fits:** Treating light as a grammar with behaviours, not just a colour. The metaphor/literal rhythm suits Dawnward's two layers: the Universe of four worlds is metaphoric, and Today's three steps are literal. The process note that they defined how it should feel before choosing any form or colour is the right order for the film too.
- **Does not fit:** The pastel blue and peach daylight palette. Airbrushed wellness gradients are the category default and would make Dawnward read as "another calm app". Sleep-state passivity also clashes, because Dawnward asks for small actions.
- **Original adaptation, "four behaviours, one gold":** Give each world in the Universe its own light *behaviour*, all in the same scarce gold on near-black, rather than a colour each:
  - Mind: a slow rack-focus from soft to sharp.
  - Body: a pulse at resting-breath tempo, about 6 per minute.
  - Soul: a low, uneven candle-like flicker.
  - Future: a horizon line that brightens from one edge.

  The viewer can tell the worlds apart by how the light moves, not by a rainbow. Use these only as the backdrop for the real Universe screen, never as fake UI.

## 3. *Power to the Pro*, BUCK for Apple (iMac Pro)

- **Client:** Apple.
- **Year:** won a Purple Pin at the Best Design Awards 2019 (New Zealand). Runtime 56 s.
- **Opened:**
  - https://designersinstitute.nz/case-study/power-to-the-pro/ (detailed case study)
  - https://buck.co/work/apple-power-to-the-pro (BUCK's page: "art film with the theme of 'power'"; "over 250 frames in the final sequence", each a "fully developed style frame"; made on the iMac Pros themselves)
- **Stills viewed:** a central black-hole disc ringed with colour, with radial grey rays; a tall, frontal, dead-centre 3D face.
- **Note:** The case study says "over 200 keyframes" and BUCK's page says "over 250 frames". These are different counts and I have not reconciled them.

### Techniques

**1. A permanent anchor shape in the centre of the frame.**
- Observation: A shape stays fixed at the centre so the eye has "a place to rest" while the rest of the frame gets busy. The team animated persistent squares and circles collapsing toward the centre first, then designed every scene on top of that wireframe.
- Source: DINZ case study. The stills I saw are both centre-weighted (radial disc, frontal face).
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: It removes the cost of searching the frame, so attention is not spent finding the subject. That matters most on a phone held at arm's length.

**2. One simulated, continuous pull-back.**
- Observation: The whole piece is one continuous camera pull-back, chosen over a fast-flying camera.
- Source: DINZ case study.
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: An unbroken camera move suggests that one thing leads to the next (cause and continuity). Pulling back reveals context and reads as "zooming out to the bigger picture".

**3. "Object permanence" transitions, and an acceleration curve.**
- Observation: An object in one scene becomes a different object in the next. The transitions speed up until, in the last 10 seconds, the wipe happens on every frame.
- Source: DINZ case study.
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: Match-transforms reward the viewer's prediction, which is satisfying because it confirms their model. Accelerating cuts raise arousal toward a climax.

### For Dawnward

- **Fits:** The central anchor is ideal for 9:16. Building the film on a wireframe of persistent shapes, then dressing it, is a strong way to link four flows (Debrief, Today, Model, Universe) into one continuous film. The pull-back maps onto "zooming out from today to your whole life".
- **Does not fit:** The maximalist mixed-style "eyegasm" and the per-frame wipes. They are the opposite of calm, and frame-rate flashing is a photosensitivity risk. Many art styles also contradict one disciplined visual system.
- **Original adaptation, "one sentence, four rooms, decelerating":** The person's own debrief sentence is the persistent object, and it stays in the centre third. Around it, the real screens change: the debrief, then the Today step drawn from those words, then the Model insight that cites them, then the Universe. Unlike the reference, the camera pull-back slows down. Each room holds a little longer than the last, so the film arrives at stillness instead of a climax. It borrows only the anchor-plus-continuity principle and inverts the energy curve.

## 4. OpenTable "Find a Restaurant" (first animated campaign), Giant Ant

- **Client:** OpenTable. Agency: Giant Ant as US creative agency.
- **Year:** 2026. The press release is dated 11 February 2026 (Valentine's Day campaign, US social).
- **Opened:**
  - https://giantant.ca/opentable-find-a-restaurant (case study and credits)
  - https://residence.prowly.com/446340-opentable-taps-giant-ant-as-us-creative-agency-debuts-first-ever-animated-campaign-for-valentines-day (press release)
- **Stills viewed:** three. A woman leaning from a car window against a dusk city; a diner at a table with a server's arm in the foreground; a 4:5 social frame of a hand dropping a twist into a cocktail.

### Techniques

**1. Reasons and feelings instead of category clichés.**
- Observation: The studio says the category is "dominated by food photos and happy people cheers-ing". This campaign focuses instead on "the reasons we gather and the feelings it creates". The press release says it was built on "simple truths" from diner insights.
- Source: case-study page; press release.
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: Recognition ("that's exactly how it feels") creates self-reference, and self-referenced messages are remembered better. Skipping the category's visual clichés helps the work stand out.

**2. Low-key frames with a single warm key light and dark foreground framing.**
- Observation: Faces are lit by one warm orange side or rim light, with large areas in deep brown-black. Foreground silhouettes, such as a server's arm or a dark shoulder, overlap the subject. One frame is an extreme close-up of hands on a small detail (the garnish).
- Source: stills seen.
- Confidence: high for these three frames. That this is the campaign-wide lighting approach is medium.
- Psychological function: Warm, low, single-source light reads as evening, intimacy and hearth. Foreground occlusion puts the viewer *in the room* rather than watching it. Close-ups of hands make small acts feel significant.

**3. An overtly hand-made line.**
- Observation: Pencil-like contour lines and textured brush fills are visible. The credits list cel animation, clean-up animation and illustration. The client describes "the sense of the artist's hand", and the studio calls it "overtly hand-made… like a great meal".
- Source: stills seen; credits; press release.
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: Effort and authorship cues signal care and quality. The hand-made quality echoes the product promise (a crafted evening) at the level of form.

### For Dawnward

- **Fits:** Dawnward's ritual is an *evening* debrief, so warm low light on a dark ground is the right emotional temperature. Starting from a human truth rather than a feature list mirrors "drawn from your own words". A 4:5 social cut exists (high: a social still on the page), which shows the visual language survives a taller crop.
- **Does not fit:** The illustrated ensemble cast and comedic register. The salmon and brown midtones. The seasonal hook. Illustrated people would also risk implying a social feature that Dawnward does not have.
- **Original adaptation, "truth before tool":** The first 5 to 6 seconds show only a typed answer to "What happened?", one honest sentence in warm off-white serif on near-black, lit as if by a single low lamp from one side (a soft warm falloff across the type). No logo, no phone frame, no feature claim yet. The product appears only after the viewer has recognised themselves. Any sample sentence must be plausible user input and be cleared in the claims ledger.

## 5. Spotify Premium product film, Oddfellows

- **Client:** Spotify (Premium).
- **Year:** not stated. The page's structured data gives a publish date of 25 September 2024 (medium confidence that this is the film's year).
- **Length:** 30 s (stated).
- **Opened:** https://oddfellows.tv/work/spotify-premium (page text, credits and stills).
- **Stills viewed:** four. Category tiles and a search bar as physical objects; the logo in a stepped dark structure; a laptop on an acoustic-foam bed; a phone on steel grating.
- **Supporting reference:** https://oddfellows.tv/work/google-fitbit. Oddfellows says its Fitbit visual language was "inspired by the light and space movement of the 1960's". That is a useful art-historical touchstone for Dawnward's light-led look. It is an illustration system, not a film.

### Techniques

**1. Product UI abstracted into tactile objects.**
- Observation: The search bar ("Playlists for any moment") and category tiles ("Wellness", "Soul", "Workout", "Party") are rebuilt as physical slabs with real finishes (speckled terrazzo, ribbing, plywood edges), shot from a high isometric angle. The page describes "abstracted product UI with tactile 3D design and animation".
- Source: page text; still seen.
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: Making an intangible service feel touchable invites imagined touch, which raises how much it seems worth. A premium tier is sold through *materiality*.

**2. A dark ground with one scarce brand accent.**
- Observation: The logo sits in a charcoal stepped well. Brand green appears only as thin glowing edges around the logo and a few step lips. On the laptop, nested dark rectangles converge on one small "Share" field outlined in green.
- Source: stills seen.
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: A single saturated accent on neutral dark wins attention through contrast (the isolation effect). Nested frames funnel the eye to one target, creating hierarchy without any text.

**3. A sensory, ASMR-led brief, with real devices in real materials.**
- Observation: The page calls it an "ASMR-inspired journey" whose aim is to "go beyond features". The phone lies on rusted steel grating, showing a simplified player UI (album art, scrubber, transport controls). Music and sound are by Zelig Sound.
- Source: page text; still seen.
- Confidence: high that the brief was ASMR-led. How it actually sounds is unknown, because I could not hear it.
- Psychological function: ASMR-style close, crisp sound narrows attention and produces calm absorption. Real surfaces ground a digital product in the physical world.

### For Dawnward

- **Fits:** Scarce accent on a dark ground is exactly Dawnward's "scarce gold". A tactile, close, quiet sound brief fits "calm, premium". A 30-second premium-tier film proves you can sell *feeling* instead of features in a short runtime.
- **Does not fit:** The multicolour pastel tiles, the playful toy-like 3D, and any abstraction that turns UI into objects the app does not have. Product fidelity requires real screens, and Dawnward must not invent physical "cards" that imply features.
- **Original adaptation, "earned gold":** Gold is spent like money, and only when the person acts: a step on Today is completed, or "That's not right" is tapped and the Model updates. Every other moment stays warm off-white on near-black. Sound follows the same rule. The only crisp, close, tactile sound is the person's own input (a key press, a breath before speaking). The film's ambience stays low and soft around it.

## 6. AirPods Pro "Quiet the Noise", Apple

- **Credits:** Agency TBWA\Media Arts Lab. Directors Megaforce. Production Iconoclast. VFX and post Electric Theatre Collective. Edit Final Cut (Joe Guest). Music: Tkay Maidza's cover of "Where Is My Mind?".
- **Year:** March 2023. About 60 s ("minute-long"; one outlet says 68 s, unverified).
- **Opened:**
  - https://itsnicethat.com/news/megaforce-tbwa-media-arts-lab-apple-quiet-the-noise-advertising-160323
  - https://www.bandt.com.au/apple-releases-calming-quiet-the-noise-spot-spruiking-airpods-noise-cancelling/
  - https://shots.net/news/view/megaforce-quiet-the-noise-for-apple
  - https://www.dandad.org/awards/professional/2024/238599/airpods-quiet-the-noise/ (D&AD 2024: two Graphite and one Wood pencil, tagged Production Design, Visual Effects and Art Direction)

### Techniques

**1. An invisible benefit made literal and spatial.**
- Observation: The noisy city (a road section with a construction worker drilling, cars, a parade, a crossing guard, a traffic jam) is lifted into the sky above one calm walker.
- Source: It's Nice That, B&T, shots, D&AD.
- Confidence: high.
- Psychological function: It externalises an internal state. "Less noise in my head" becomes something you can see, so you understand the benefit before any copy explains it.

**2. An on/off toggle inside the story.**
- Observation: When she turns noise cancelling off to buy a juice, the bustle comes back down. When she turns it on again, the noise goes back up.
- Source: B&T.
- Confidence: high for the toggle. The order of events is from B&T's summary only.
- Psychological function: A before/after inside one continuous scene, triggered by the user, ties the change to the product (causal attribution). It also shows the user is in control.

**3. Stripped-back sound and a lyric that doubles as theme.**
- Observation: The soundtrack is a "stripped-back" cover of "Where Is My Mind?".
- Source: It's Nice That; B&T; shots credits.
- Confidence: high for the credit and "stripped-back". How the mix behaves at the toggle is unknown, because I could not hear it.
- Psychological function: When the lyric and the idea say the same thing, meaning lands twice through two senses. Sparse music leaves room for the "quiet" to be felt.

### For Dawnward

- **Fits:** Dawnward also makes an inner state legible: a noisy day becomes three small steps. The toggle principle maps onto a real, distinctive control, "That's not right". A single protagonist across about 60 s is the right scale.
- **Does not fit:** Big-budget live-action VFX spectacle. A licensed pop song. Urban comedy. The floating-city image is iconic and must not be imitated in any form (no lifting or floating of objects).
- **Original adaptation, "agency is the toggle":** The proof moment is the person correcting the app, not the app performing. Show a real Model insight, the "Why I think this" evidence, then a tap on "That's not right". The insight's evidence visibly re-weighs or withdraws. **Depict only the behaviour the shipped app actually performs; verify it with the product-fidelity check first.** The before/after is about the person's authority, which is the "no guilt, it shows you why" promise demonstrated rather than claimed.

## Five cross-cutting principles for a 60 s, 9:16 Dawnward ad

1. **Anchor the eye in the centre third and let the world change around it.** On a phone held at arm's length, the subject should never be hunted for. Keep one persistent element, ideally the person's own sentence, fixed while screens and light change around it. *(Power to the Pro anchor; Calm Sleep's centred symmetry; Distance's centred Morse line.)*
2. **Light is the protagonist, and gold is earned.** Use a near-black ground with warm off-white for everything else. Gold appears only on a human action, roughly one gold event per beat and never as ambient decoration. Give each of the four worlds a light *behaviour*, not a colour. *(Distance; Spotify's single accent; Calm Sleep's light behaviours; OpenTable's single warm key.)*
3. **Truth before tool.** Spend the first 3 to 6 seconds on a recognisable human sentence, then reveal the product. Insight-led openings beat feature-led openings for both recognition and memory. *(OpenTable "reasons we gather"; Spotify "beyond features".)*
4. **One through-line, decelerating.** Join Debrief, Today, Model and Universe with an object that persists across them (the person's words) and one continuous camera idea. Slow the pacing down toward the end, so the film lands in stillness, which matches "calm" and gives the end card room. *(Power to the Pro, with its curve inverted.)*
5. **Make the invisible felt, then prove it with the real UI.** Allow one restrained metaphor, then cut to the shipped screen that makes it true ("Why I think this", "That's not right"). Keep the visible hand in grain and texture, and keep sound close, tactile and sparse. *(Quiet the Noise's toggle; Calm Sleep's metaphor/literal rhythm; Distance and OpenTable's visible hand; Spotify's ASMR brief.)*

## Three things to avoid: they read as generic or AI-made

1. **The default "AI understands you" iconography.** Glowing neural meshes, particle brains, glass orbs, smooth textureless gradient blobs and chat-bubble swarms. They signal a template, not a point of view. Replace them with real Dawnward screens, real words, and grain or hand-made texture in the light.
2. **Metrics, rings and the sunrise cliché.** Progress rings, counters, charts, "Day 7" badges and confetti contradict "no streaks, no scores, no guilt". Also avoid the literal stock "dawn": a sunrise over mountains, a person smiling at a phone in bed, a hand-holding-phone lifestyle B-roll. The brand name does not need illustrating.
3. **Uniform, meaningless motion.** Every element on the same ease-in-out curve, constant slow camera drift, morph transitions that link nothing, kinetic-type buzzword montages ("Grow. Reflect. Evolve."), a synthetic voiceover and a stock piano swell. Hierarchy should come from timing contrast: long holds, one decisive move, and silence before the important line.
